# Tijuana clinics prostate cancer therapies

*Research compiled 22 October 2026. Not medical advice. Anyone with prostate cancer should review these options with a urologist or oncologist before acting.*

## Bottom line

- **Every clinic uses a similar menu.** It combines IV infusions (high-dose vitamin C, curcumin, laetrile/"B17", herbal IVs), heat (hyperthermia or oncothermia), oxygen therapies (ozone), lab-grown immune cells (dendritic-cell "vaccines" and NK cells), and diet and detox (juicing, coffee enemas, supplements). Most also add emotional and spiritual support.
- **Prostate-specific material is thin.** Oasis of Hope has a prostate page listing IV vitamin C, IV curcumin, metabolic therapy and its "ImPACT" immune-cell therapy. Hope4Cancer markets its program as a way to avoid surgery, radiation and hormone therapy and preserve urinary and sexual function. CHIPSA points to Apatone (vitamin C + K3), which was tested in a small, uncontrolled prostate study. None of the seven clinics publishes independently verified prostate outcome data.
- **No clinic modality has shown that it controls prostate cancer.** The best of them, Apatone and clinic-made dendritic-cell vaccines, have only small early-phase data. Laetrile has no supporting trials and can cause cyanide poisoning, and that risk is higher when it is combined with high-dose vitamin C, which some clinics do.
- **The measures with real prostate evidence are low-cost and can be done at home.** They are active surveillance for low-risk disease, regular exercise, and a healthful plant-based diet. They are covered in the last section.

### How this was researched, and its limits
A multi-agent deep-research workflow ran 5 search angles and 45 agents. The clinic websites (oasisofhope.com, hope4cancer.com, chipsahospital.org) could not be fetched from this research environment because of DNS/proxy blocks. Clinic details below therefore come from search-engine extracts of those pages, medical-tourism directories (WhatClinic, PlacidWay), patient fundraisers and journalism, not from full-page reads. Only one claim set passed the workflow's strict 3-vote verification: the Coley's toxin case report. Treat clinic modality lists as **"clinic says,"** and confirm them directly with any clinic before relying on them.

## What the independent evidence says, for prostate cancer specifically

| Modality | Best evidence | Prostate-specific | Main safety concerns |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Laetrile / amygdalin (B17)** | Cochrane review: **no randomized trials**; claims "lack sound clinical support"; risk–benefit is **negative** ([Cochrane CD005476](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD005476_laetrile-treatment-cancer)) | None | **Cyanide poisoning**, made worse by **high-dose vitamin C**, which Oasis-style protocols combine with it |
| **High-dose IV vitamin C** | Systematic review of 23 trials (385 patients), mostly single-arm phase I/II: generally safe, **no phase III efficacy data** ([Antioxidants 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6071214/)) | **Apatone** (vit C 5 g + K3 50 mg orally, 12 weeks) in 17 men with rising PSA: PSA rise slowed in 13/17. **No control group** ([Tareen, Int J Med Sci 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2288789)) | Hemolysis if G6PD-deficient; kidney stones; can distort blood-glucose readings |
| **Gerson / juicing / coffee enemas** | NCI: only flawed retrospective reports; NCI reviews in 1947 and 1959 found no convincing efficacy ([NCI PDQ](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK65971/)) | None | Coffee enemas linked to **infections, electrolyte imbalance, colitis, deaths** |
| **Dendritic-cell vaccines** | 2025 systematic review: phase I/II data show immune responses, but protocols vary widely ([PMC12112211](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12112211/)) | The only approved DC-type product is **sipuleucel-T (Provenge)**, FDA 2010, for metastatic castration-resistant disease; it gave about a 4-month survival gain in its pivotal trial. Clinic-made vaccines are **not** sipuleucel-T | Unregulated lab manufacturing: contamination and infection risk |
| **NK-cell infusions** | Clinical effectiveness of NK therapy in humans "has yet to be confirmed" ([ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07202611)) | None | Same manufacturing risks |
| **Coley's toxins** | Historical (1890s–1900s) bacterial therapy for sarcomas; **not approved** for any indication | One 2022 case: an 80-year-old's metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer regressed after **accidental life-threatening sepsis**. Authors say it was a first-ever report, causation unproven, with other explanations possible (metformin, radiation) ([Cureus 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9838081/)). **This was the only claim verified 3–0 in this research** | Deliberately induced fevers and infection |
| **Hyperthermia** | Legitimate **only alongside radiotherapy** in some settings; no level 1–2 evidence for hyperthermia alone ([NAFKAM summary](https://nafkam.no/node/15884)) | 2022 systematic review covers heat combined with radiation for prostate cancer | Whole-body heating above 106 °F risks burns, cardiac strain and organ injury |
| **Insulin potentiation therapy (low-dose chemo)** | 2 small trials and 4 case reports; no survival data ([NAFKAM](https://nafkam.no/node/15884)) | One uncontrolled 16-patient castration-resistant prostate study | Dangerous hypoglycemia; underdosed chemo may promote resistance |
| **Mistletoe** | Cochrane: 21 studies, none high-quality; too weak for conclusions ([Cochrane CD003297](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD003297_mistletoe-treatment-cancer-patients)) | None | Injection-site and flu-like reactions |
| **Sono-photodynamic therapy** | No published controlled human trials | Contrast: **TOOKAD (padeliporfin)** vascular-targeted photodynamic therapy is a real, trial-tested treatment for low-risk prostate cancer (EU-approved; FDA declined) | Photosensitivity |
| **Poly-MVA** | "No medical evidence that Poly-MVA confers any health benefit" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poly-MVA)) | None | Cost (about $19,800/year reported in 2004) |
| **Ozone** | No controlled cancer trials | None | Embolism and infection from autohemotherapy |

**Supplement cautions for prostate patients.** These come from general knowledge of the trials, not this run's sources.
- In the **SELECT** trial, **vitamin E raised prostate cancer risk** by about 17%.
- **PC-SPES**, a once-popular prostate herbal, was withdrawn in 2002 after it was found contaminated with prescription drugs, including DES and warfarin.
- In a placebo-controlled trial, **pomegranate extract** did not significantly slow PSA rise compared with placebo.
- Some herbs interact with **abiraterone, enzalutamide, docetaxel** and radiation. Tell your oncologist about every supplement and IV you receive.

**Outcomes when patients skip standard care.** In a US National Cancer Database study, patients who chose alternative medicine alone had about **2.5× higher risk of death** overall ([Johnson, JNCI 2018](https://academic.oup.com/jnci/article/110/1/121/4064136)). The **prostate subgroup showed no significant difference**, most likely because many early prostate cancers grow slowly enough that surveillance alone does well. For that group, the risk of a Tijuana program is mostly cost and harm, not lost survival. For aggressive or advanced prostate cancer, the gap matters.

**Historical scrutiny of border clinics.** The American Cancer Society documented misdiagnosis at border clinics: healthy people told they had "precancer," and patients with active disease told they were cured ([CA Cancer J Clin 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1878788/)). Al Jazeera's 2012 undercover investigation found many clinics operating under **palliative-care licenses** while promising cures ([AHCJ](https://healthjournalism.org/blog/2012/01/survivor-goes-undercover-in-tijuana-cancer-clinics/)). Baja regulators closed all or part of more than a dozen clinics, but enforcement faded for lack of inspectors ([California Healthline](https://californiahealthline.org/morning-breakout/san-diego-uniontribune-examines-controversial-alternative-cancer-clinics/)).

## The "holistic" measures with real prostate evidence

These are what a patient seeking a non-toxic, whole-person approach can do with good data behind it:

1. **Active surveillance (low- and some intermediate-risk disease).** The **ProtecT** trial followed men for 15 years: prostate cancer death was about **3% whether monitored, operated on or irradiated**. Monitoring roughly doubled metastasis risk, and 61% of monitored men eventually had treatment ([NEJM 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10876753/)). This is the evidence-based way to avoid immediate surgery or radiation.
2. **Intensive lifestyle program.** In the Ornish RCT, 93 men on surveillance followed a plant-based diet, exercise, stress management and group support. **PSA fell 4% vs. rose 6% in controls** after 1 year, and at 2 years 2/43 vs 13/49 had gone on to conventional treatment ([UCSF](https://www.ucsf.edu/news/2005/08/101429/lifestyle-and-diet-may-stop-or-reverse-prostate-cancer-progression)). It was small, but it is the closest real-world analogue to a "holistic program."
3. **Healthful plant-based diet.** In the CaPSURE cohort of 2,062 men, those in the top fifth for a healthful plant-based diet had **47% lower risk of progression** than the bottom fifth. The data are observational ([JAMA Netw Open 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38691361/); [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/may-2024/plant-based-diet-may-offer-benefit-in-patients-with-prostate-cancer/)).
4. **Exercise.** The **ASCO 2022 guideline** recommends aerobic and resistance exercise during treatment. Prostate is one of the most-studied cancers ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20220919/exercise-should-be-standard-part-of-cancer-care-during-treatment-asco-guideline-states)). The same guideline found **insufficient evidence for ketogenic diets, fasting and supplements**. **INTERVAL-GAP4** is testing whether high-intensity exercise extends survival in metastatic prostate cancer ([UTS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/173327)).
5. **Evidence-based immunotherapy and focal options.** Sipuleucel-T, and trial-tested focal therapies (HIFU, cryotherapy, TOOKAD photodynamic therapy) where appropriate, are the regulated versions of what the clinics advertise.

## If someone is still considering one of these clinics
- Get a **standard work-up first**: biopsy Gleason/Grade Group, PSA, PSMA-PET or MRI. Many men with low-risk disease can safely do surveillance plus lifestyle changes at home, for a tiny fraction of $45–60K.
- Ask the clinic in writing for: its **COFEPRIS license** and whether it is a hospital or palliative license, **prostate-specific outcome data** with PSA and imaging, an itemized price, the **source and lab of any cell products**, and whether it uses **laetrile together with vitamin C**.
- Keep the US urologist or oncologist informed, and keep monitoring PSA and imaging throughout.
